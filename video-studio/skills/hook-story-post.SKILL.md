# Hook, Story, Next Step

A framework for posts that earn attention and stay accurate. Rule above all others: reach is only useful if the message is true and kind. Catchy but inaccurate loses trust. Accurate but boring loses the reader.

## Before writing

1. Identify the brand and load its rules (see Brand profiles). For a brand not listed, ask for its audience, voice, colours and call to action.
2. Identify the reader and the one question or belief they already have. Prefer a real question the audience asks (search tools such as AnswerThePublic, comments, client conversations) over an invented one.
3. Pick one content type and one format.

## Step 1: Hook (the first three seconds)

Write three hooks, one of each type, then pick the strongest:

- **Question:** the question the reader already has.
- **Surprise:** a fact that breaks an expectation.
- **Bold statement:** a clear, true claim.

Checks: it sparks curiosity without fear or shock. You can answer it honestly in one sentence. It makes one promise.

## Hook formulas (the Postiz Write brief library)

The three hook types above are the families. These 21 formulas are the specific shapes. Pick the formula from what you can honestly say: a formula that needs a number, a result or a rule you follow is only allowed when that number, result or rule is real. Fill the template, then check the trap.

| # | Formula | Template | Best for | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contrarian Take | Everyone says {common advice}. After {specific experience}, I think that is wrong. | Establishing a point of view. | Being contrarian about something nobody believes. The belief has to be genuinely common. |
| 2 | Number Reveal | I {did thing} for {N} {days/weeks}. Here is what actually happened. | Experiments and anything with a countable result. | Burying the number. Put it in line 1 or the formula does nothing. |
| 3 | Mistake Confession | {Specific cost} is what {one mistake} cost me. | Lessons learned the hard way. | Confessing something small. The cost has to be real. |
| 4 | Before / After | {Time ago} I was {low state}. Today I {high state}. The difference was {one thing}. | Transformation with one clear cause. | Several causes. Pick the one that matters. |
| 5 | The List Promise | {N} things I wish someone had told me before {milestone}. | Practical lessons people will save. | A list with no reason to keep reading. Each item must earn its place. |
| 6 | Insider Secret | After {N} years {doing thing}, here is the part nobody puts in the {job posting/course/pitch}. | Behind-the-scenes insight. | A secret everyone already knows. |
| 7 | The Callout | If you are still {doing outdated thing}, stop. | Direct, opinionated advice. | Calling out people, not practices. |
| 8 | Question Trap | {Specific scenario with a real cost}. What do you do? | Opening a real debate. | A vague question. Only you could ask this one. |
| 9 | Story Cold Open | "{Line of dialogue}" {Who said it, when, and why it mattered}. | Stories with a turn. | Dialogue that never gets to its point. |
| 10 | The Receipt | {Screenshot or hard number}. {One sentence of context}. | Proof, when you can show it. | Showing a number with no context. |
| 11 | Myth Bust | {Popular explanation} is not why {bad outcome} is happening to you. | Correcting a common belief. | Dismissing a belief without giving the real cause. |
| 12 | The Comparison | {Expensive option} vs {cheap option}. {Surprising verdict}. | Helping someone decide. | A comparison that is not fair to both sides. |
| 13 | Permission Slip | You are allowed to {thing the reader feels guilty about}. | Releasing guilt and encouraging a decision. | Permission for something harmful. |
| 14 | Pattern Interrupt | {One word or three-word line, full stop.} | Grabbing attention in a crowded feed. | A line that means nothing without the post. |
| 15 | The Warning | {Common practice} is quietly costing you {specific thing they care about}. | Pointing out a hidden cost. | A vague cost. Name it. |
| 16 | Good vs Great | Good {role} {do X}. Great {role} {do Y}. | Raising the standard. | Making the good version a straw man. |
| 17 | Time Anchor | {Task} used to take me {long time}. It now takes {short time}. | Efficiency and process. | A time with no way to show how. |
| 18 | The Unpopular Rule | I do not {widely accepted practice}. Ever. Here is why. | Stating a rule you actually follow. | A rule you do not follow. |
| 19 | Curiosity Gap | The {superlative} {thing} I ever {verb} {broke the obvious rule}. | Making readers want the end. | A gap the post never closes. |
| 20 | The Walk-Away | I {fired/quit/deleted/cancelled} {valuable thing}. {Result}. | Showing a bold decision. | A decision with no result. |
| 21 | The Direct Value | Here is the exact {artifact} I used to {specific outcome}. Steal it. | Giving something useful. | A template that is generic. It must be the real one. |

Formulas that need real proof before use: Number Reveal, Mistake Confession, Before / After, Insider Secret, The Receipt, Time Anchor, The Unpopular Rule, The Walk-Away, The Direct Value. Without the proof, choose another formula. "No hook" is also valid: open with the point.

## Formats

- **LinkedIn post:** 900 to 1,300 characters. Line 1 is the hook and must work alone. Line 2 is the payoff, not setup. Paragraphs of one to three lines. One idea, one takeaway, then one question or one instruction, never both. No links in the body.
- **Carousel:** six to ten slides, one idea per slide. Slide 1 is the hook in as few words as possible. Each slide makes the reader want the next. The last slide gives one takeaway and one instruction.
- **Article:** 800 to 1,500 words. Open with a real moment, then the framework. Three sections at most. Close with one step for this week.
- **Newsletter issue:** 700 to 1,200 words. Subject line under 50 characters. Story, framework, one step. End with one question.

## Story structures

- **Human entry, then the system:** a real moment, the pattern beneath it, the reframe, one practical step, one question to carry forward.
- **Problem, cause, fix:** the problem the reader feels, why it keeps happening, what changes it, how to start this week.
- **Before, shift, after:** where I was, the shift, where I am now with proof, what the reader can do.
- **Story, lesson, question:** the story in two or three lines, the lesson in one sentence, one question.

## Responding to someone else's idea

Credit the person by name on the slide or in the post. Do not screenshot or copy their post. Say what you agree with, then where you differ, then add your own points. Do not repeat their historical or factual claims as your own unless you have checked them.

## Step 2: Story (question to answer)

A story is a short journey from a question to an answer, in three parts:

| Part | Job | Also called |
|---|---|---|
| Beginning | What the reader already believes or asks | Myth |
| Turn | The moment something changes: the fact, in plain words | Truth |
| Ending | The answer, and something the reader can do today | Next step |

Never skip the turn. It is the part that changes the reader's mind.

## Step 3: Next step

End with one real action the reader can take today. One action per post. Never promise something that does not exist yet (a template, a link, a result).

## Content types

1. **Educational:** tutorials, myth-busting, quick tips. Watch for facts too dense to read.
2. **Storytelling:** a personal or origin story in three parts.
3. **Authority and social proof:** case studies and testimonials. Real ones only, with consent.
4. **Episodic series:** a repeating format people come back for. Plan 8 to 10 ahead.
5. **Interactive:** polls and questions.

## Carousel framework (10 slides, 1080x1350)

| Slides | Job |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hook |
| 2 to 3 | Build interest with a real situation the reader recognises |
| 4 to 5 | Keep attention with one simple diagram |
| 6 to 9 | Practical information, one point per slide |
| 10 | Call to action |

Three slide types: **hook** (one headline, one image, a clear promise), **value** (one point, a little text, a lot of visuals), **CTA** (the creator's photo and one simple action). If a value slide has too much text, cut it in half. Shorter sets keep the same order: hook, interest, practical, CTA.

Carousel formats: comparison, tutorial, native (your own face or work), compilation, story.

Design: 2 fonts (one bold headline, one clean body), 3 colours (background, text, one accent), 1 image style on every slide.

## Final check (score each 1 to 5)

1. **Accuracy:** every claim is true and can be backed up. No invented results, numbers, clients or testimonials.
2. **Respectful language:** nobody could be identified without consent, no group is blamed, and a reader who is struggling would feel safe sharing it.
3. **Hook strength:** it gives a reason to stay in three seconds.
4. **Clarity of the next step:** one real action.

Fix anything below 4 before delivering. Show the user the draft and get approval before publishing or scheduling. Saving as a draft is fine when the user asks for it.

## Carousel visual system (learned from the user's reference samples)

Apply the techniques, not the reference creator's branding or content.

- **Background:** deep and dark, never flat. Add a soft colour glow in one corner, a faint dot grid and fine grain. For BuildWithJA this is deep purple (#210044 base, glows toward #5b1aa0).
- **Every slide has two strips:** top (small logo left, pill page counter "3/10" right) and bottom (name and title left, swipe pill or next-slide teaser with an arrow chip right).
- **Hook slides:** a huge, tight, heavy headline (about 100px, letter-spacing -0.045em). Put the key phrase in a design-tool selection box: accent border, four corner handles, a small tag above, a cursor with a name tag. End it with an accent-coloured full stop.
- **Value slides:** lighter two-tone heading (weight 600, one phrase in the accent), a numbered accent chip, one short paragraph, and a row of small progress cards with the current one filled in the accent.
- **Info cards:** translucent rounded cards (radius about 22px) with a tiny uppercase label and a bold value.
- **Real references:** show the actual screenshot in a rounded card with a border and shadow, slightly rotated, with a label beside it saying what it is. Use it on the cover when the post replies to someone.
- **CTA slide:** the creator's photo in a rounded card, one question, one pill button.
- Rounded corners and pill buttons throughout. One accent colour.
